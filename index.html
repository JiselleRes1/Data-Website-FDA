Which Movie Genres Actually Pay Off?

By Jiselle Resendiz

This report analyzes 72,775 movies released between 1950 and 2025, drawn from TMDB's full catalog, across 19 genres. Each movie carries its genre, release year, language, runtime, critic rating, and — where TMDB reports it — its production budget and box-office revenue. The headline finding: cheaper genres often out-earn expensive ones on a per-dollar basis, and the genres audiences and critics rate highest are rarely the genres that make the most money back on their budget.

About this data

Source
Full TMDB Movies Dataset (asaniczka, Kaggle), scraped from The Movie Database API, downloaded 2026-09-27.
What one row is
One row is one movie with a "Released" status on TMDB, identified by its release date and its primary genre.
Rows dropped and why
From ~1.5 million raw rows: kept only status == "Released"; dropped rows with an unparseable release date or a release year outside 1950–2026 (earlier dates in the raw file are data-entry errors); dropped rows with no listed genre (took the first listed genre as each movie's primary_genre when a movie lists several); dropped rows with fewer than 10 TMDB votes, since the raw file is dominated by obscure, essentially unrated entries that would otherwise swamp every genre-level average. Result: 72,775 rows. See scripts/clean_data.py for the exact code.
How rates and averages are computed
Yield = revenue ÷ budget, computed only for movies with both budget > 0 and revenue > 0 reported (8,645 of 72,775 movies — TMDB doesn't have real financial data for most titles, so a budget or revenue of 0 means "not reported," not "zero dollars"). Genre-level yield uses the median (not the mean) to avoid a handful of outlier blockbusters/flops dominating the number, and is only reported for genres with at least 30 financially-reported titles. All other averages (rating, popularity, runtime) are means over the full 72,775-row dataset unless stated otherwise.
